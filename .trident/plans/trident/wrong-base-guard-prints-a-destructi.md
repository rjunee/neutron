# IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN — the wrong-base guard resolves its remedy instead of guessing it (PR #494)

Card: the wrong-base refusal printed an unconditional `git branch -D` remedy that would have destroyed a live lane's work (and, in the case the guard actually fires on, often could not even run). Fix: before composing the refusal, resolve the HOLDER (live worktree / dead-owner worktree / none / unknown) and the publication state (local sha vs `refs/remotes/origin/<branch>` after a one-ref fetch), then emit exactly ONE evidence-naming refusal, chosen by that resolution. The card names four required arms and the as-built ships them all — live holder (no `branch -D` anywhere in the message), dead holder (unlock-then-remove), unheld-and-published (the delete, with both shas as the reason it is safe), unheld-and-unpublished (salvage, never delete) — plus the shapes the four do not cover, each with its own arm rather than being folded into a neighbour it would misdescribe: the holder is the repo's OWN shared checkout (no other lane to wait for), the holder is a PRUNABLE entry whose directory is gone, a worktree mid-rebase/mid-bisect holds the branch without the listing saying so, and every UNKNOWN (holder, publication, or the ancestry the outer guard itself could not establish). UNKNOWN never authorises an irreversible act. The refusal itself is never weakened.
