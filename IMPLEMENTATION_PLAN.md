# IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN — the wrong-base guard resolves its remedy instead of guessing it (PR #494)

Card: the wrong-base refusal printed an unconditional `git branch -D` remedy that would have destroyed a live lane's work (and, in the case the guard actually fires on, often could not even run). Fix: before composing the refusal, resolve the HOLDER (live worktree / dead-owner worktree / none / unknown) and the publication state (local sha vs `refs/remotes/origin/<branch>` after a one-ref fetch), then emit exactly ONE evidence-naming refusal, chosen by that resolution. The card names four required arms and the as-built ships all four — live holder (no `branch -D` anywhere in the message), dead holder (unlock-then-remove), unheld-and-published (the delete, with both shas as the reason it is safe), unheld-and-unpublished (salvage, never delete) — WITH ONE STATED PRECONDITION ON THE SECOND, because two reviewers measured it and the acceptance claim has to match what runs (Argus round 9 major): the DEAD-holder arm is gated behind an occupancy cross-check, and `probeTreeOccupancy` answers UNKNOWN if ANY `/proc/<pid>/cwd` on the box is unreadable — so off euid 0, on a host carrying other users' processes, a dead lock pid always degrades to the UNKNOWN arm and the DEAD arm is reached only where this process may read every /proc entry (root, or a single-uid box) or from injected fixtures. That degradation is deliberate and in the SAFE direction — an unreadable /proc entry may be a process standing in the tree, and `clear` is the answer that would print `worktree remove` — and the UNKNOWN arm it degrades to names the dead pid, says why occupancy could not be established, prints the read that settles it and authorises nothing. So: three arms fire in production at non-root euid, four where /proc is fully readable, and nothing about that is hidden from the reader of the refusal — plus the shapes the four do not cover, each with its own arm rather than being folded into a neighbour it would misdescribe: the holder is the repo's OWN shared checkout (no other lane to wait for), the holder is a PRUNABLE entry whose directory is gone, a worktree mid-rebase/mid-bisect holds the branch without the listing saying so, and every UNKNOWN (holder, publication, or the ancestry the outer guard itself could not establish). UNKNOWN never authorises an irreversible act. The refusal itself is never weakened.
